## Reading (descriptive, not adoptive)

- The aggregate gap (≈0.05) is a *count* effect, not a quality effect: had
  GeneNMF emitted 7 MPs at the same per-program quality it would lead. That is
  the rank-selection problem in another costume — GeneNMF never selects rank,
  so it cannot win an aggregate that prices program count. This was predicted
  in SOURCE_AUDIT §2.4 ("no sense in which GeneNMF chooses K better or worse").
- Per-program, donor-separated factorization recovers cleaner gene sets than
  pooled consensus top-50s here — but without a selection rule those sets come
  in an unpriced bundle of 10, and no margin was frozen to price them (P4-01
  froze hyperparameters only, adoption deliberately off the table).
- Consistent with, and independent of, the B-DROP: at 24-donor DEVELOPMENT
  scale, donor-aware discovery shows no advantage by either family's metric.

## Decisions

- Comparator status: **COMPLETE AS DESIGNED** (native workflow executed,
  converted, scored; provenance recorded).
- Scientific adoption: **INCONCLUSIVE** — and structurally so: no margins were
  frozen because no adoption was ever on the table, and the two readings
  (aggregate vs per-program) point opposite ways. This gate touches no cNMF
  claim: A/B/C verdicts, the baseline recommendation, and the sealed tier
  stand exactly as M1-04 left them.

## Supported / unsupported scope

Supported: the numbers above with the asymmetries stated (cNMF read at true
rank; GeneNMF pooled across K and donors; single seed/dataset per scenario).
Unsupported: any "GeneNMF is better/worse than cNMF" claim, any rank-selection
inference, any real-data or confirmation-tier statement.
